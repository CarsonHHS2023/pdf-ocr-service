## 1. Status

M7 is Planned. M7 begins only after the applicable M4 Structured Content / Structured Document Foundation, M5 Reader MVP, and selected M6 Smart Reading Intelligence foundations are stable enough for archive planning and implementation slices to depend on them. Smart Archive is a peer application, not the owner of canonical document content. M7 may use selected shared retrieval and intelligence foundations, but generated artifacts must remain distinguishable from original content. This plan does not authorize implementation, provider or index selection, an external pilot, release, production claims, or commercial readiness claims. Final M7 scope and any future Smart Archive Foundation / Smart Archive Intelligence split require later reviewed decisions.

## 2. Milestone goal

Deliver the smallest coherent Smart Archive application that organizes and retrieves documents through shared Structured Content, provenance, evidence, metadata, and lifecycle foundations; supports traceable archive workflows and evidence-backed cross-document surfaces; and preserves clear ownership, retention, deletion, and original-versus-generated-content boundaries.

M7 is not a complete enterprise content-management platform, and minimum M7 completion does not require full Archive Intelligence.

## 3. Historical relocation from former M5

Roadmap v2 placed Smart Archive in M5. Roadmap v3 decomposed the delivery sequence so M5 became Reader MVP, M6 became Smart Reading Intelligence, and Smart Archive moved to M7. The earlier Smart Archive plan remains historical evidence for archive intent and scope. Its scope was rescheduled after shared content, Reader, and selected intelligence foundations; it was not declared failed, cancelled, invalid, abandoned, or obsolete.

## 4. M4–M6 dependencies and starting point

M7 depends on M4 providing stable Structured Content / Structured Document identity, accepted/current-content lifecycle, source/page/node/asset evidence anchors, provenance chain, recovery state, migration/compatibility rules, and deterministic content fixtures.

M7 may depend on M5 providing Reader-compatible document access, navigation/location identity, lexical search behavior, supported document-type declarations, asset presentation references, and ownership/security context where applicable.

M7 may depend on selected M6 foundations providing evidence and citation policy, semantic retrieval/index foundations if selected, generated-content provenance, safe-answer behavior, cost/privacy/security controls, and explicit distinction between original and generated content.

M7 must not treat provider JSON, Raw Processing Result, SPR, Reader serialization, legacy Reader tables, generated summaries, Q&A responses, Flashcards, Mind Maps, Tutor responses, or semantic index entries as canonical archive truth.

Repository implementation evidence currently shows source retention, Raw Processing Result handling, SPR normalization, foundation database records, Reader-facing compatibility routes, storage design, tests, and CI coverage. It does not prove implemented archive collections, cross-document retrieval, archive lifecycle, semantic archive search, or archive AI.

## 5. Archive architecture boundary

```text
Source Evidence
→ Raw Processing Result
→ Structured Processing Result
→ Structured Content / Structured Document
→ shared metadata, provenance, evidence, and retrieval foundations
→ Smart Archive collections/workflows
→ archive retrieval and evidence-backed surfaces
```

Structured Content remains the accepted document-content boundary. Archive metadata is application metadata, not replacement content. Retrieval indexes are derived and rebuildable. Archive organization does not redefine canonical document structure. Generated intelligence remains derived. Reader and Smart Archive are peer applications over shared foundations. Direct provider JSON-to-archive coupling is not target architecture.

## 6. Required M7 scope

### A. Archive item identity

M7 must define conceptual archive identity for an archived document or item, source/content version, ownership, archive location or collection membership, lifecycle state, provenance linkage, and deletion/retention state. Final table or class names remain deferred.

### B. Collections and folders

M7 must plan minimum organization behavior for collections, folders, or another approved organization model; membership and movement; ordering where required; duplicate or repeated membership policy; ownership/access boundaries; deletion behavior; and the rule that folders do not define canonical document hierarchy. The exact organization model remains a decision.

### C. Archive metadata

M7 must address title and descriptive metadata, source metadata, tags or classifications if selected, dates and ownership, supported document-type information, processing/recovery state, provenance, user-entered versus extracted/generated metadata distinction, and metadata version/update behavior. M7 must not build a universal metadata ontology.

### D. Archive lifecycle

M7 must define conceptual lifecycle states such as available, processing, partially available, unavailable, archived, removed, retained, pending deletion, deleted where permitted, and failed or recovery-required. Exact state names are not finalized here.

### E. Retrieval

M7 must include deterministic metadata filtering, lexical retrieval, document/item lookup, collection/folder browsing, supported semantic retrieval only if inherited from approved M6 foundations, result-to-document/evidence linkage, stale-index handling, and no fabricated retrieval results.

### F. Cross-document evidence surfaces

M7 may plan evidence-backed surfaces across documents. Every factual generated or synthesized claim must cite applicable evidence; original document evidence remains traceable; insufficient evidence produces no-answer or limited output; conflicting evidence is represented safely; generated synthesis is labelled; and cross-document output is not canonical archive content. Cross-document AI is not required for minimum M7 completion unless selected.

### G. Archive workflows

M7 must plan minimum workflows to add/import an already supported document, organize, browse, search, open in Reader, inspect provenance/recovery state, move between collections, remove from a collection, archive or restore, request deletion, rebuild derived indexes, and handle partial failure. Final UI is not defined here.

### H. Ownership and access boundaries

M7 requires a user or tenant ownership decision, collection/item access control, private/shared behavior if selected, no cross-tenant retrieval leakage, generated intelligence obeying the same boundaries, and auditability where required. The final auth provider is not selected here.

### I. Retention and deletion

M7 must require policy for source evidence, Raw Result, SPR, Structured Content, projections, indexes, Archive metadata, generated artifacts, caches, user state, and backups. Removing from a collection is not automatically physical deletion. Archive deletion must respect legal, operational, and evidence-retention constraints. Derived indexes and generated artifacts must be invalidated or deleted when required. Exact retention durations remain deferred.

### J. Provenance and evidence

M7 must preserve traceability from archive surfaces to Document, SourceFile, content version, applicable processing evidence, selected/generated metadata source, retrieval/index version, and generated feature provenance where applicable. No provider secrets may be persisted.

### K. Recovery and degraded-content behavior

M7 must surface or respect partial documents, no-usable-semantic-content pages, missing assets, unsupported formats, stale processing, failed indexing, unavailable source evidence, and content-versus-processing failure distinctions. Archive availability must not imply complete content quality.

### L. Index and rebuild discipline

M7 requires indexes linked to content/metadata versions, indexes that are derived and rebuildable, stale-index detection, deletion and ownership propagation, migration strategy, deterministic or testable rebuild behavior, and no index as sole source of truth. No final search or vector technology is selected.

### M. Import and duplicate behavior

M7 must define decisions for repeat upload/import, the same source under multiple collections, duplicate detection, replacement versus new version, imported content ownership, and source/content identity preservation. M7 must not silently deduplicate without an approved rule.

### N. Large-archive bounded behavior

M7 requires bounded listing and search, a pagination/cursor/chunking decision, predictable handling of large collections, index and query cost controls, explicit loading/error states, and performance measurement for supported archive workflows. This plan does not set arbitrary final SLAs.

### O. Reader and intelligence integration

M7 requires archived documents to open through M5 Reader boundaries. M7 must not duplicate Reader content models. Selected M6 intelligence appears only through approved derived surfaces. Generated outputs are labelled and version-linked. Archive can function without requiring every M6 feature.

## 7. Explicit non-goals

M7 excludes ownership of canonical Structured Content; provider JSON as archive truth; Raw Result or SPR as direct archive product content; Reader serialization as canonical archive content; duplicating Reader content models; generated summaries, Q&A, Flashcards, Mind Maps, or Tutor responses as canonical archive truth; mandatory implementation of full cross-document AI; uncited cross-document RAG; a final universal enterprise records-management platform; a complete legal-hold system; a complete collaboration/community product; final billing/subscription; final AI provider; final embedding model; final vector/search database; final metadata ontology; final auth provider; final deployment target; public/commercial release without the external gate; deciding the M7 Foundation/Intelligence split now; and universal document-format support.

## 11. Completion / exit criteria

M7 may be Complete only for its explicitly approved minimum scope. All criteria below are Required / Not yet demonstrated:

1. The minimum M7 scope is explicitly recorded.
2. The Foundation/Intelligence split has a recorded decision, including a decision not to split yet.
3. Archive items link to approved Structured Content / Structured Document identity.
4. Smart Archive does not own or duplicate canonical document content.
5. Organization behavior is deterministic for the approved collection/folder model.
6. Metadata distinguishes extracted, user-entered, and generated values where applicable.
7. Archive lifecycle and ownership states are explicit and tested.
8. Reader access uses the approved M5 boundary.
9. Provider JSON, Raw Result, SPR, Reader serialization, and legacy tables are not canonical archive truth.
10. Lexical retrieval is deterministic for the approved scope.
11. Semantic retrieval, if selected, is version-linked, rebuildable, and ownership-safe.
12. Cross-document generated claims, if selected, cite relevant evidence and support safe no-answer behavior.
13. Generated intelligence remains labelled and noncanonical.
14. Retention and deletion behavior is documented and tested across source, content, metadata, indexes, caches, and generated artifacts.
15. Removing collection membership is distinguished from deleting source or content.
16. Stale/deleted content does not silently remain active in retrieval indexes.
17. Import, duplicate, and version behavior is explicit and tested.
18. Recovery/degraded states remain visible and are not represented as complete content.
19. Large archive workflows do not rely on unbounded listing or search responses.
20. Ownership and tenant boundaries prevent unauthorized retrieval.
21. No provider secrets or sensitive data leak into metadata, indexes, or logs.
22. Tests cover organization, metadata, lifecycle, retrieval, ownership, deletion, rebuild, duplicate behavior, degraded states, and selected intelligence behavior.
23. M7 limitations and deferred scope are documented.
24. Internal demonstration evidence is distinguished from external release readiness.
25. External pilot/commercial claims remain blocked unless the separate gate is satisfied.
26. Completion evidence is recorded before Milestone Status changes.

## 12. Validation strategy

Future validation must include archive identity/lifecycle tests; collection/folder organization tests; metadata-source distinction tests; import/duplicate/version tests; lexical retrieval tests; semantic retrieval/index tests if selected; evidence/citation tests for cross-document surfaces if selected; Reader integration tests; ownership/authorization isolation tests; retention/deletion/cleanup tests; stale-index and rebuild tests; recovery/degraded-state tests; large-archive bounded-behavior tests; invalid/loading/failure-state tests; no-secret-leak checks; migration tests where applicable; link/document consistency review; and controlled internal workflow demonstrations.

One successful demo does not complete M7. Search quality alone does not prove archive correctness. An index is not the source of truth. Implementation and tests remain future work.

## 13. Archive metadata and lifecycle policy

M7 requires metadata source classification, content-version linkage, ownership, lifecycle state, update history where needed, generated metadata labelling, recovery/quality indication, explicit deletion/retention relationship, and no silent replacement of original content. Exact schema remains deferred.

## 14. Retrieval and evidence policy

M7 requires deterministic lexical retrieval for approved fields, semantic retrieval only if selected, indexes derived from approved content/metadata, ownership filtering before results are exposed, result-to-evidence linkage, stale-index detection, no fabricated result, no-answer for unsupported cross-document synthesis, and citations for factual generated claims where applicable.

## 15. Retention, deletion, ownership, and privacy policy

M7 requires an explicit ownership boundary; a clear difference among removing, archiving, retaining, and deleting; propagation to caches, indexes, and generated artifacts; source-evidence retention constraints; privacy/provider-data handling; backup/restore interaction; auditability where required; no cross-tenant leakage; and safe behavior for partial deletion failure. Final durations and legal rules remain deferred.

## 16. Generated-intelligence treatment

Generated outputs remain derived. The original/generated distinction remains visible. Generation provenance is retained where applicable. Stale generations are invalidated after source/content changes. Generated artifacts obey ownership and deletion. Archive does not silently promote generated artifacts to canonical content. Later acceptance of generated artifacts as content requires a separate lifecycle decision.

## 17. External pilot/commercial gate

Before external users or commercial claims, M7 requires authentication, authorization, user/tenant ownership, security review, reliable processing and archive status, retry/idempotency, secure storage/deployment, search/provider quotas and cost controls, observability, backup/restore, retention/deletion policy, privacy expectations, provider data-use review, incident/failure recovery, support expectations, generated-content disclaimers where applicable, and tested cross-tenant isolation.

This gate is not Milestone Status. Passing it does not automatically authorize release. Controlled internal demos may precede it. M7 may complete internally without commercial release if milestone criteria are satisfied.

## 18. Future split decision

Roadmap v3 permits considering a later split into Smart Archive Foundation and Smart Archive Intelligence. This M7 plan does not accept that split automatically. A split should be considered only if intelligence scope, delivery risk, or measurable exit criteria justify it. The decision must preserve M7 historical continuity. No M8 or renamed milestone is created by this document.

| M7 expands into a full enterprise platform | Scope and exit criteria become unreachable | Approve smallest coherent scope first | Minimum scope recorded |

| Reader and Archive content models diverge | Inconsistent user surfaces | Use M5 Reader boundary | Reader integration tests |
